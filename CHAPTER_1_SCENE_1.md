# Chapter 1 — Scene 1: The Spirit-Devouring Pearl

> **Status: AUTHOR-APPROVED SCENE.** The events and details presented in this scene are established canon. Future revisions require explicit author approval.

“You’ve always had a talent for making a spectacle of your own failure, Tianxing.”

The voice was a cold rasp, cutting through the haze of incense and blood. Jiang Tingyuan opened his eyes to find himself slumped against a mahogany pillar, his silk robes torn and stained. Standing five paces away was a man whose expression suggested he was looking at a particularly repulsive insect. This was Elder Wei, the clan’s disciplinarian, and according to the narrative trajectory of the novel, the man currently overseeing Gu Tianxing’s public humiliation before the final, lethal blow was dealt.

Tingyuan didn’t gasp. He didn't cry out in confusion. He simply looked at the bruise forming on his ribs and then back at the Elder. The spatial layout of the room clicked into place: the Great Hall of the Gu Clan, the high dais, the circle of mocking cousins. He was precisely where the original Gu Tianxing had been when he’d tried to "prove" his cultivation by attempting to absorb a Spirit-Devouring Pearl—a task far beyond his current level, resulting in a spiritual backlash that had effectively lobotomized the original owner of this body.

*The Pearl,* Tingyuan thought. He looked down. Resting on the polished floor between him and the Elder was a sphere of iridescent violet, pulsing with a rhythmic, predatory light. In the novel, this was the catalyst. The protagonist, currently a lowly servant in the kitchens, was supposed to witness this failure, feel a flicker of pity, and later use a hidden technique to reclaim the Pearl from the wreckage of Gu Tianxing’s life.

Tingyuan’s mind, honed by decades of corporate warfare, didn't see a magical artifact; he saw a leveraged asset. The original Gu Tianxing had failed because he tried to force the Pearl into his core through sheer arrogance. But Tingyuan knew the "hidden" logic of this world’s cultivation—the same logic the novel’s author had hinted at in the appendices. The Pearl didn't respond to strength; it responded to a void.
