## 1. Project Overview

**Title:** The Villain Who Read the Ending  
**Genre:** Cultivation, reincarnation, villain protagonist, strategic progression  
**Workflow:** Vault → one-scene outline → Perchance prose → review → author approval → merge approved prose into the chapter file → update this Bible  
**Current progress:** Chapter 1 contains two author-approved scenes and is stored as one continuous chapter in `THE_VILLAIN_WHO_READ_THE_ENDING.md`.

### Core premise

Jiang Tingyuan, a ruthless self-made Chinese corporate mastermind, builds a dominant global technology empire through learned competence, ruthless competition, manipulation, questionable dealings, and acquisitions. After finishing a cultivation novel he considers predictable and clichéd, he dies in a car crash involving a freight truck while his personal driver is driving.

He awakens in the novel's world as Gu Tianxing, a young master of the Gu Clan. The original Gu Tianxing is an arrogant, incompetent disposable villain who is destined to die early so the original male protagonist can look impressive. Jiang retains his own memories and personality alongside memories and foreknowledge from the original novel. He intends to survive and control his future rather than passively follow the plot.

### Core story promise

A corporate mastermind applies strategic thinking, leverage, resource management, manipulation, and opportunism to a cultivation world whose rules he must learn. His knowledge of the original novel is an advantage, not omniscience. His interference can make remembered outcomes unreliable.

## 6. The Original Cultivation Novel

### Established
- Jiang finishes reading a cultivation novel shortly before dying.
- He considers its story predictable and its protagonist and villains clichéd.
- He believes that, if placed in that world, he could act more strategically than its characters.
- The original male protagonist uses a newfound technique in a way that makes Gu Tianxing a disposable foil.
- The original protagonist eventually becomes the world's greatest cultivator and defeats the Azure Dragons, a calamity beyond the cultivation world's comprehension.
- Jiang remembers the plot and has foreknowledge of some events, opportunities, hidden treasures, and the broad trajectory of the story.
- The extent and reliability of his knowledge have not been fully defined.

### Undecided
- The novel's title, author, exact length, and complete plot.
- The original protagonist's name, personality, background, abilities, and relationship with Jiang.
- Which hidden treasures Jiang remembers, their locations, their rules, and whether he can reach them.
- The precise original death scene for Gu Tianxing.
- Whether Jiang regards the original protagonist as a rival, asset, target, or something else.
- How much of the original plot Jiang can accurately recall.

## 8. Knowledge, Advantages, and Limitations

### Jiang's established advantages
- Memories and experience from his first life, including corporate strategy and ruthless decision-making.
- Memories of the cultivation novel, including its broad plot and some opportunities and future events.
- The body and social identity of Gu Tianxing, a young master of the Gu Clan.
- Some foreign information about Gu Tianxing, including names, faces, and clan politics, which arrives without replacing Jiang's own memories.

### Constraints
- Jiang must learn the actual rules and mechanics of cultivation.
- Foreknowledge is limited to what he remembers or can infer from the original novel.
- He can misremember, misunderstand, or make incorrect assumptions.
- Changes caused by his own interventions can make future events unpredictable.
- Intelligence and strategic experience do not make him physically invincible or automatically competent at cultivation.

### Not established
- A system, status screen, quest interface, or system voice.
- A special reincarnation gift beyond his retained memories and foreknowledge.
- A unique bloodline, constitution, artifact, or innate cultivation talent.
- Any specific cultivation realm, ranking system, technique, or supernatural ability.

Do not introduce these as canon without author approval.

## 12. Themes and Writing Principles

- Jiang values competence, control, leverage, strategic advantage, and results.
- His ruthlessness should not be reduced to empty cruelty or theatrical villainy.
- His arrogance should come from confidence in his capabilities, not the original Gu Tianxing's mindless entitlement.
- Demonstrate intelligence through observation, judgment, planning, adaptation, and consequences rather than repeatedly declaring him a genius.
- His corporate background should inform his decisions without making every thought sound like a business presentation.
- Prioritize strong hooks, immediate stakes, concrete problems, and consequential decisions.
- Avoid prolonged exposition before the reader has a reason to care.
- Avoid repetitive internal monologues that merely restate that Jiang is calculating.
- Keep major cultivation mechanics and worldbuilding open until the story needs them.
- Distinguish established canon from Jiang's assumptions and from details not yet defined.

## 13. Perchance Scene Input Format

Each outline must cover **one scene only**, targeting approximately five paragraphs of generated prose.

Use this format:

# Chapter [number] — [Chapter Title] — Scene [number]: [Scene Label]

**Scene Purpose:** What this one scene needs to accomplish.

**Outline:**
- Events in intended order.
- What the viewpoint character observes, decides, attempts, or learns.
- Relevant conflict and consequences.
- The scene's ending state.

**Characterization:** How the characters should think and behave.

**Tone:** Pacing, mood, and prose direction.

**Scene Ending:** The specific beat on which the scene should end.

**Continuity Constraints:**
- Approved facts that must remain consistent.
- Unresolved questions that must not be silently answered.
- Any prohibited inventions or premature resolutions.

Do not put multiple scenes in one Perchance input. The outline should be presented in one easy-to-copy block. After generation, review the prose before updating canon.

## 15. Author Approval Policy

The author has final authority over all canon. For every scene:
1. Prepare one-scene outline using the approved format.
2. Generate prose in Perchance.
3. Review for hook, pacing, characterization, continuity, and invented mechanics.
4. Make no silent edits to approved prose.
5. Once the author approves a scene, append its exact text to the correct chapter in `THE_VILLAIN_WHO_READ_THE_ENDING.md`, using a scene break where appropriate. Do not create a separate file for each scene.
6. Update this Bible and its continuity ledger/change log in the same workflow.
7. If an approved scene contains a detail that conflicts with earlier canon, flag it and ask whether the author wants a deliberate revision rather than silently choosing one version.

**Current operating principle:** Build the story incrementally. Keep the approved prose intact, keep undecided details open, and let each new scene earn its place in canon.
